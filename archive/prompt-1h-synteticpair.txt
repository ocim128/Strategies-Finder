DATA-CONSTRUCTION FACTS (read before generating anything)
The synthetic series is a RATIO, not a sum or average. For each aligned source-time bar:
- open  = base.open  / quote.open
- close = base.close / quote.close
- high  = max(open, close, base.high/quote.high, base.low/quote.low)
- low   = min(open, close, base.high/quote.high, base.low/quote.low)
- volume = min(base.volume, quote.volume)   // a constructed proxy, not an identified leg's liquidity

The loader may build the ratio from a finer divisible source interval and aggregate it to 1H/2H. In that path, target-bar high/low are extrema of the generated ratio sub-bars and target-bar volume is the sum of their synthetic volumes. Timestamp alignment and data availability can also vary by source.

Design consequences:
1. The ratio's absolute level depends on the two leg price scales. Do not use fixed raw-price thresholds; express portable triggers with returns, relative ranges, rolling ranks, or normalized deviations.
2. Do not assume that a ratio is inherently mean-reverting or trend-following. Treat reversion, continuation, and coupling as hypotheses to test on the generated series.
3. Bar range is the range of the derived ratio. It may reflect movement in either leg and, because the OHLC values are combined from same-time leg extrema, it is not a direct measurement of either leg's volatility or of leg disagreement.
4. Synthetic volume is a constructed `min(base.volume, quote.volume)` proxy at the source interval. It does not identify either leg's liquidity and is not tradable volume. Use it only through a normalized relative transformation, and do not interpret it as absolute effort, size, or capital capacity.
5. A ratio breakout is an observation, not proof of persistence. Require an explicitly defined hold or acceptance condition only when the proposed mechanism and data support it; validate that condition empirically.
6. No additional rebase should be assumed. Any strategy that needs a fixed price level or a familiar price range is not portable across generated pairs.
